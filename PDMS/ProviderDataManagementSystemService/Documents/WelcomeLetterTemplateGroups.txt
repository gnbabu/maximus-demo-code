<p><b>Electronic Visit Verification for Providers of Long-Term Services and Supports</b></p>

<p>Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) uses technology to document certain <a href="https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/medicaid.ohio.gov/Providers/EVV/Providers/ODM_EVV_Covered_Programs_and_Services.pdf">services</a> in the home or community by electronically recording the time, date, and detail of services provided to members.</br></br><a href="https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/chapter-5160-32">Ohio Administrative Code Chapter 5160-32</a> informs providers of EVV requirements. The Ohio Department of Medicaid has partnered with a vendor to implement an EVV solution. While you have completed the required EVV training prior to enrollment as a Medicaid provider, access to the EVV system and the capture of visits is required before claims for services subject to EVV may be paid. </br></br>It is important that you create an EVV account on the <a href="https://evv-registration.sandata.com/">EVV self-registration portal</a> if you haven't already done so. Please contact the EVV help desk at 855-805-3505 with any questions on getting started with EVV. See the <a href="https://medicaid.ohio.gov/resources-for-providers/special-programs-and-initiatives/electronic-visit-verification">Ohio Medicaid EVV webpage</a> for helpful information on EVV. </p>

<p><b>Changes to Provider Information</b></p>

<p>In accordance with Ohio Administrative Code 5160-1-17.2, you are required to notify ODM within 30 days of any change to your provider information. In order to have a successful partnership, it is important that ODM has complete and accurate information. To ensure we have updated information, you are responsible for updating your demographic information, such as your address, and phone number, by logging onto the secure ODM Web Portal. Should anything else change, for example your tax ID, licensure, certification, or group membership information, you need to notify the ODM Provider Enrollment Unit within 30 days of the change. It is essential that your enrollment information is current in order to process claim transactions appropriately and report accurate income information to both you and the federal government. We appreciate your cooperation with this important requirement</p>

<p><b>Records</b></p>

<p>Medicaid providers are required to maintain clinical and administrative records for a six-year period. These records must document medical necessity and indicate the need for the type, extent, and level of the service you provided for the patient. These records may be requested by ODM for audit purposes and will be reviewed to assure program compliance. Please note, in the event of a unfavorable audit finding, you may be required to repay ODM for reimbursement received that lacked required supporting documentation for services deemed medically necessary.</p>

<p>General information for Medicaid providers, provider manuals that govern Ohio Medicaid, billing instructions, contact information and addresses, provider training schedules and registration can be found on our website at <a href="[[Medicaid_URL]]">[[Medicaid_URL]]</a>.</p>

<p><b>Direct Deposit of Payment</b></p>
<p>As of January 1, 2013, most providers are required to receive payments via EFT, in accordance with ORC 5164.46. For those providers, failure to enroll for EFT will result in non-payment of claims.</p>

<p>Instructions on how to enroll for Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) can be found on ODM’s website at <a href="[[Medicaid_URL]]">[[Medicaid_URL]]</a>. Once at the website, under the Provider tab, select Billing and then Direct Deposit. For assistance navigating the website, visit <a href="[[Medicaid_URL]]">[[Medicaid_URL]]</a>. For Information about scheduled training events and links to materials click <a href="[[PDMS-URL]]">here</a>.</p>

<p>Individuals who work for a group and are reimbursed through the group rather than reimbursed directly from Medicaid, do not need to sign up for an EFT.</p>
